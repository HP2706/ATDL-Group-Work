\section{Decision and correction of the earlier estimate}
We have selected \emph{Deep Double Descent: Where Bigger Models and More Data Hurt}.
This document inventories every numbered figure in the locally stored, 32-page
2021 journal version, including its appendices. It is a planning audit, not the
final four-page assignment submission. Figure numbers refer to that version;
they should not be silently transferred to another arXiv or ICLR revision.
The previous comparison of 18 candidate papers is preserved unchanged as
\href{paper-selection-cost-report.pdf}{\texttt{paper-selection-cost-report.pdf}},
with source \texttt{paper-selection-cost-report.tex}.

\textbf{The previous 56 GPU-hour / \$195.44 image-only core and 120 GPU-hour
translation allowance do not price reproduction of this paper.} They were
untimed allowances for reduced experiments, with neither the full run grids
nor sufficient modality coverage. In particular, language translation is a
central part of the evidence for model-wise and sample-wise double descent,
not a minor optional add-on. No earlier runtime should be treated as measured.

\textbf{An exact GPU-hour bill is not established by the available evidence.}
We verified paper protocols, public result-table counts, and selected released
width/sample grids. We did not run training or a GPU timing pilot, and the
inspected sources do not supply hardware-specific wall times. Accordingly,
this report provides exact conditional arithmetic where possible, explicit
unknowns elsewhere, and sensitivity calculations rather than fabricated
benchmark numbers. There is no verified all-figures total or guaranteed
\$1,000 full reproduction.

\section{Tasks, modalities, and common protocol}
\textbf{Image classification:} CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with five-layer CNNs
and preactivation ResNet18 variants. Widths scale as $[k,2k,4k,8k]$.
The CNN has four convolution/BatchNorm/ReLU/pooling stages and a final
classifier; $k=64$ has 1,558,026 parameters (P, Appendix B.1).
Default image batch size is 128. Adam uses learning rate $10^{-4}$ for
4,000 epochs; SGD generally uses 500,000 updates, initial learning rate
0.1, no momentum, and inverse-square-root decay updated every 512 steps.
CIFAR-100 CNN experiments in Figures 7/20 instead use one million steps.
Augmentation is padded random crop and horizontal flip. Noise is sampled
once per training example, replacing its label with a uniformly random
\emph{incorrect} class; it is not resampled each epoch. Weight decay and
dropout are absent unless explicitly introduced.

\textbf{Neural machine translation:} IWSLT'14 German-to-English, approximately
160,000 training sentence pairs; its 4,000/18,000-pair subsets and a larger
sample-count sweep; WMT'14 English-to-French subsampled to 200,000 pairs.
The paper specifies the six-layer encoder--decoder Transformer with eight
attention heads, embedding dimension $d$, feed-forward width $4d$, 10\%
label smoothing, no dropout, BPE preprocessing, and 80,000 updates.
Appendix A identifies Adam, and Appendix B.3 refers to the Transformer
warmup/inverse-square-root schedule. The image batch-size statement must not
be assumed to define the translation token batch. Measure per-token
held-out loss/perplexity with teacher forcing; BLEU generation is not a
substitute for these figures.

\textbf{Random features:} Fashion-MNIST with a fixed Gaussian random Fourier
feature map, a zero-initialized linear readout and MSE/gradient flow.
This is a separate estimator family on images, not another language or audio
modality. Speech/TIMIT, SVHN and other tasks in the related-work discussion
are experiments from other papers, not additional experiments in this one.
The paper's non-image modality is text translation in two language pairs.
Adversarial image training, regularization and ensembling are additional
experimental families, not separate input modalities.

\section{GPU-hour accounting used throughout}
Let $t_i$ be measured seconds per optimizer update, $U_i$ the update count,
$g_i$ the number of GPUs used by that run, and $e_i$ its evaluation,
checkpoint, initialization and interruption overhead in GPU-hours. Then
\[
 H=\sum_{i\in\text{unique runs}}\left(g_iU_it_i/3600+e_i\right),
 \qquad C=\sum_i r_i H_i+C_{\rm CPU,storage,transfer}.
\]
Two GPUs for ten elapsed hours cost 20 GPU-hours. Repeated metric plots from
one trajectory are not new training runs. Independent seeds, noise masks,
subsets or optimizers are new runs. Reuse requires matching these as well as
all hyperparameters and the full training horizon.

For compact per-figure accounting, the following are \emph{training-only}
GPU-hours per run on one GPU. Every $t$ is in seconds/update and depends on
width, dataset, precision, batching and hardware; it is not constant across
the real sweep.

$A$ assumes $\lceil50000/128\rceil=391$ batches/epoch; dropping the final
batch changes this to 390. $P$ assumes 196 batches/epoch. Here $t$ for $P$
includes all ten PGD inner attack steps and the outer training update;
ordinary SGD timing cannot be substituted. More GPUs require multiplication
by $g$, using the measured distributed step time. For a sweep, sum the
appropriate function separately over widths and seeds. Products such as
$320A$ below are shorthand using a sweep-average per-run cost.

\textbf{Sensitivity, not measured bounds:} at $t=0.01/0.1$ s, $A$ costs
4.34/43.44 GPU-h, $S$ costs 1.39/13.89 GPU-h and $L$ costs 2.78/27.78 GPU-h.
At $t=0.1/0.5$ s, $T$ costs 2.22/11.11 GPU-h. At a complete adversarial
update time of $0.1/1$ s, $P$ costs 4.36/43.56 GPU-h. These pairs are
hypothetical timing scenarios, not confidence intervals, forecasts, or B200
measurements. A width-1 CNN and a width-128 CNN should never receive one
unqualified four-hour runtime.

For continuity with the archived report, dollar examples use
\textbf{\$3.49/GPU-hour as a historical scenario rate}, not a current verified
quote. The provider pricing page was not retrievable during this audit.
Requote the actual GPU, billing granularity and minimum GPU allocation before
booking. CPU preprocessing, storage, transfer, checkpoint retention, idle
rental, evaluation and failed/restarted work remain additional costs.

\textbf{Metric/provenance caveats discovered in the released data.}
The model-wise notebooks label the vertical axis cross-entropy, while the
paper captions call it perplexity. Some CSVs have identical train-error and
test-error columns, and the 18k error column uses a different numerical scale
from several other tables. The notebook legend also reverses the language
directions relative to the paper. These discrepancies do not alter row
counts, but the files must not be used uncritically as validated metrics.
Record loss units and log base before transforming to perplexity; use clean
held-out negative log-likelihood rather than the label-smoothed training
objective for a newly implemented evaluation.

\section{Image and auxiliary experiments: what can be priced now}
\textbf{The Figure 1 grid alone:} 64 widths and five trials imply 320 runs.
At 4,000 epochs and 391 batches/epoch that is 500,480,000 updates.
An assumed sweep-average 0.01 s/update costs 1,390.22 GPU-h and
\$4,851.88; at 0.1 s/update it costs 13,902.22 GPU-h and \$48,518.76.
These are conditional examples, not measured requirements, but they expose
why seven widths on a reduced dataset cannot price the original figure.

\textbf{The Figure 12 sample/width grid:} the nine released sample sizes are
3,125; 4,419; 6,250; 8,839; 12,500; 17,678; 25,000; 35,355; and 50,000.
Its 42 widths give 378 settings per repeat if every cell is required and
valid. With 500k updates each, $t=0.01$ s implies 525 GPU-h
(\$1,832.25); $t=0.1$ s implies 5,250 GPU-h (\$18,322.50).
The five-repeat statement for Figure 11(a)'s lower panel is not evidence
that the entire Figure 12 grid has five repeats.

\textbf{Optimizer/schedule checks:} the 24 visually identified settings in
Figures 16--18, each extended to one million updates, imply $24L$ per
repeat: 66.67/666.67 GPU-h at 0.01/0.1 s. Width and dynamic-drop parameters
still need recovery. Learning-rate curves that stop early in the plot do
not establish a common completed horizon.

\textbf{Random features and parameter counting:} CPU-only choices require
zero GPU-hours, but dense feature matrices and least-squares factorizations
can dominate RAM and CPU time. Exact dimensions, numerical method and
regularization/gradient-flow stopping must be specified and timed. Do not
reserve a GPU merely because the previous cost table used one as a proxy.
A large solve belongs on suitable remote compute.

\textbf{Adversarial training:} counting 800 epochs alone misses ten PGD
steps inside each update and separate robust evaluation. The ambiguous
36-width released archive would imply 72 runs for two radii only after its
architecture and condition mapping are resolved. It is not legitimate to
price this as two ordinary image sweeps.

\textbf{Ensembles:} five members per width, with the specified independent
corruptions for Figure 28. Preserve per-example predictions. The paper's
mean/standard-deviation curves and a CSV of aggregate losses cannot recover
plurality-vote ensemble errors. If members or predictions are missing,
training or inference is additional work.

\textbf{No grand total is added across figure rows.} Many rows are views of
shared trajectories, while several grids and seed counts remain unknown.
A defensible full total requires a union of run configurations, not the
sum of all displayed figure costs. The verified grids already show that the
full paper is materially larger than the previous proposed course subset.

\section{Conflicts that must remain visible}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
\item Appendix B.1 says ``80 gradient steps'' for Transformers, whereas
Section 4 and Figure 8 say 80k. This audit uses 80,000 and records the conflict.
\item Appendix B.4 says 500k \emph{epochs} for Figure 11(a); the main SGD
protocol is 500k \emph{updates}. Costs here use updates provisionally, not
500k full dataset passes. Original run configurations are needed to settle it.
\item Appendix A has figure mappings inconsistent with captions, including
Figure 25 and Figure 21's noise level. Caption-specific settings are used
provisionally: Figure 21 is 10\% noise; Figure 25 is augmented SGD at 10\%.
\item Figure 29's reference to Figure 7 conflicts on CIFAR-10 versus
CIFAR-100. They are not counted as the same training job.
\item The README calls translation optimization SGD; Appendix A says Adam
and B.3 references the Transformer recipe. This audit follows the paper
provisionally and does not claim released training code confirms it.
\item The adversarial archive says \texttt{mcnn}; Figure 26 says ResNet18.
The decay archive says Adam while E.2.2 specifies SGD. Directory names are
not enough to resolve either mapping.
\item Figure 1 evaluates against a noisy test distribution; other figures
use clean test targets. For ten classes and noise probability $p$, clean
error $e$ gives expected noisy error $p+(1-10p/9)e$. The inspected plotting
notebook instead uses an expression with the opposite sign on its final
$ep/9$ term. Check against original data before claiming exact curve recovery.
\item Optimal early stopping in published plots is an oracle envelope over
test trajectories. A new practical early-stopping experiment must use a
validation split. Figure 19 is an explicit exception to the broad appendix
heading claiming early stopping does not exhibit double descent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{A costable course subset, without concealing its reductions}
A useful cross-modality candidate is one image width/dynamics experiment,
one translation width/sample experiment and one independent ablation. This
is a proposed menu, not an approved compute launch or a claim that it
reproduces every figure.

\textbf{Image contribution:} seven pilot-selected ResNet widths, CIFAR-10
50k, 15\% noise, augmentation, full 4k epochs, three seeds: 21 runs.
It targets Figures 1/2 but uses fewer widths and repeats. A clean-label
ablation at three of those widths adds nine runs. Both epoch-wise and
model-wise plots use the same saved histories. Total: $30A$, or
130.33/1,303.33 GPU-h at hypothetical 0.01/0.1 s per update.

\textbf{Translation contribution:} six pilot-selected dimensions (multiples
of eight) at both 4k and 18k pairs, three seeds: 36 runs at 80k updates.
It targets Figures 3/23 and a sparse cross-section of Figure 11(b), with
explicitly reduced grid density. Total: $36T$, or 80/400 GPU-h at
hypothetical 0.1/0.5 s per update. A separate WMT comparison is not included;
adding six WMT widths and three seeds costs another $18T_{\rm WMT}$.

The image sweep, translation sweep and clean-label ablation give three
concrete experimental contributions without pretending image-only work
covers the text results. At the faster illustrative timings, the combined
$30A+36T$ is 210.33 GPU-h or \$734.06 before overhead; doubling that amount
is \$1,468.13. At the slower illustrative timings it is 1,703.33 GPU-h or
\$5,944.63 before overhead. \textbf{Even this subset cannot yet be promised
under \$1,000.} Fewer seeds, fewer widths, smaller image subsets or shorter
horizons require a new explicit scope decision; stopping before interpolation
can remove the phenomenon being tested. Pilot results must determine the
choice rather than retroactively justifying an attractive price.

\section{What is required to turn this into a measured quote}
The missing step is a small remote timing pilot on the actual rentable GPU,
after an executable training path has been verified. No ML training or
benchmark should run on the local Mac. A pilot should cover a small,
near-interpolation and large width for each chosen architecture and dataset;
translation needs both language pairs if both are included. Fix vocabulary,
sequence lengths, tokens per update, gradient accumulation, numerical
precision, evaluation cadence and checkpoint policy before timing.

Measure warmup separately, then multiple steady-state windows; record
seconds/update, examples or tokens per second, peak VRAM, GPU count and model,
software versions, complete configuration, source/data hashes and seed.
Time a complete validation pass and a checkpoint write separately. Verify
that resuming restores optimizer, schedule, RNG and sampler state, so
preemption does not force repeated training. Save training/validation/test
histories with their exact steps and independent-noise identifiers.

The run ledger must contain one row per unique
(dataset/split, sample subset, architecture, width, optimizer, schedule,
noise seed, augmentation, weight decay, training seed, horizon) tuple, with
its target figures. Calculate each row with its own measured throughput and
sum once. Record setup, data processing, storage, transfer, inference and
interruption costs separately; re-price the chosen provider allocation.
Until that ledger and the missing source configurations are resolved,
\emph{the protocols and counts above are auditable, but an exact final
GPU-hour cost remains unverified}.
